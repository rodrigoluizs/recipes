---
title: Spaghetti Carbonara
servings: 2
prepTime: 10 min
cookTime: 20 min
ingredients:
  - amount: 200
    unit: g
    name: spaghetti
  - amount: 100
    unit: g
    name: guanciale
    note: or pancetta, cut into strips
  - amount: 3
    name: eggs
  - amount: 140
    unit: g
    name: pecorino romano
    note: grated
  - name: black pepper
    note: to taste
  - amount: 1
    unit: tbsp
    name: coarse salt
    note: for the pasta water
method:
  - Bring a large pot of water to a boil with the [[coarse salt]]. Add the [[spaghetti]] and cook according to the package instructions. Just before it's done, scoop out a big mug of the pasta water and set it aside.
  - In a skillet, fry the [[guanciale]] over medium heat until crispy.
  - Whisk together the [[eggs]], [[pecorino romano]], and [[black pepper]] in a bowl until you get a thick, smooth cream.
  - Using tongs, transfer the spaghetti directly from the pot to the skillet with the guanciale — no need to drain it — along with a splash of the reserved pasta water. Let some of the water evaporate, then turn off the heat and let it rest for 20 seconds.
  - The egg mixture will be a bit cold; temper it by stirring in about half a ladle of the reserved pasta water.
  - After the pasta has rested for 20 seconds, turn the heat back on to low-medium and add a bit more pasta water plus the egg mixture. Toss everything together for 2 minutes.
  - Serve immediately, finishing with more [[black pepper]].

A classic Roman carbonara: guanciale rendered crisp, an egg-and-pecorino cream
tempered off the heat, and just enough starchy pasta water to bring it all
together into a silky sauce with no cream involved.

## Tips

- Watch the heat once the egg mixture goes in — it should thicken into a
  cream, not scramble. Keep the pan off heat or on very low while tossing.
- The guanciale should finish crisping up around the same time the pasta
  reaches al dente.
