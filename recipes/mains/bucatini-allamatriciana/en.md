method:
  - Cut the [[guanciale]] into cubes, keeping the fat attached.
  - Pour the [[peeled tomatoes]] into a bowl and crush them with a fork.
  - Bring the [[water]] to a boil with a generous handful of [[salt]], for cooking the pasta.
  - In a skillet, cook the guanciale over low-medium heat, stirring occasionally, until crispy.
  - Once the guanciale turns golden and crispy, add the [[white wine]] and stir.
  - After about 3 minutes, once most of the wine has evaporated, stir again and add the crushed tomatoes.
  - Simmer over low heat for 10 to 15 minutes.
  - Meanwhile, cook the [[bucatini]] in the boiling water until al dente, following the package instructions. Just before it's done, scoop out a big mug of the pasta water and set it aside.
  - Once the sauce has reduced, turn off the heat and season with [[salt]] and [[pepper]] to taste.
  - Using tongs or a spider, transfer the pasta directly from the pot to the skillet — no need to drain it.
  - Add a few spoonfuls of the reserved pasta water and toss well, adding more if needed to loosen the sauce. Serve immediately.

A Roman classic: guanciale rendered crisp, deglazed with white wine, then
simmered with crushed tomatoes into a sharp, savory sauce that clings to
bucatini.

## Tips

- Cook the guanciale low and slow first — rushing it keeps the fat from
  rendering out properly and it won't crisp up.
- Save more pasta water than you think you'll need; it's what loosens the
  sauce back up once the pasta goes in.
